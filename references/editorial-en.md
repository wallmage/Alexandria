# Evidence-safe English edit

Edit for a clear, direct, human voice.

- Lead with the point.
- Give each section a job: answer a reader question, explain the evidence or mechanism, test a material limit, and state the consequence.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Cut throat-clearing, repetition, and generic transitions.
- Vary sentence length when the idea warrants it.
- Replace slogans and vague praise with observable facts.
- Keep technical language when it is precise; explain it once.
- Use headings that tell the reader something.
- Avoid canned openings, forced contrasts, fake quotations, and ornamental metaphors.
- Treat serial enumeration as a warning sign. Keep coordinate lists to the few
  items the reader must retain; a sentence with five or more comma-separated
  items needs a table, grouping, or a cut. Across a report, allow no more than
  three such sentences per 1,000 words.
- Do not repeat the `X is not Y. It is Z.` frame or semicolon antithesis as a
  house rhythm. Use either construction at most twice in a report.
- Do not turn uncertainty into certainty.
- Do not add facts, examples, or color that the evidence ledger does not support.
- Use concrete cases or worked examples where they materially improve understanding; do not add decorative anecdotes.
- End analytical sections on the implication, not every paragraph. Most
  paragraphs should finish with their last fact or explanation; do not add an
  epigram merely to manufacture weight.
- Preserve the report’s hard length range by replacing repetition with deeper evidence, mechanisms, counterevidence, comparisons, or implications.
- State a decision once at the executive level and once where the evidence is
  synthesized. Merge buyer profiles, checklists, and pilot instructions when
  they ask the reader to evaluate the same dimensions.
- If a number is material enough to invoke, state it and cite it. Do not tell
  the reader that a published range exists while withholding the range.
- Define product labels and technical terms at first use. Remove names that do
  not change the reader's understanding or decision.
- Put a citation in every paragraph that makes a platform-specific, numerical,
  legal, policy, or other externally verifiable claim. A bibliography entry
  alone does not make nearby prose traceable.

Read the draft once for the argument, once for value density, once for evidence fidelity, and once aloud for rhythm. If a smooth rewrite changes a number, attribution, qualifier, citation, or causal claim, restore the verified meaning.
