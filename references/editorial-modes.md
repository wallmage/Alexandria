# Editorial modes

Choose at most one dominant mode. Use its craft traits; do not imitate a living writer's distinctive voice.
Record the chosen mode in the evidence ledger brief so the final content
review can test the report against the mode actually used.

## Analytical

Best for companies, technologies, markets, and policy. State the judgment early, compare alternatives on reader-relevant criteria, quantify when evidence permits, make assumptions visible, and end with a usable decision rule.

## Narrative

Best for people and events. Build around consequential scenes that sources document, then connect them to the larger explanation and judgment. Never invent dialogue, setting, motive, or chronology.

## Explanatory

Best for concepts and systems. Move from a concrete example to the mechanism, then test the model against exceptions, competing explanations, and limits.

## Investigative

Best for disputes and accountability. Separate allegation, documentary evidence, response, and independent verification. Give the subject's strongest supported answer, not a token rebuttal, and state what remains unknowable.

## Historical

Best when sequence and change explain the present. Use period-appropriate sources, identify hinge points without pretending history was inevitable, and distinguish contemporary interpretation from later scholarship.

## Critical

Best for works, products, and ideas. Describe the object accurately before judging it. Define the criteria, compare credible alternatives, and show where reasonable readers may disagree and what evidence would change the assessment.
