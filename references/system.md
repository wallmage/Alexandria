# System Archetype

Research framework for markets, industries, ecosystems, economies, geopolitical orders, trends, social phenomena, cultures, cities, regions, or any complex, ongoing, multi-actor domain that doesn't reduce to a single entity or event.

## When to Use

The subject is a complex, dynamic system — not a single thing, person, or event, but an ongoing web of interacting forces, actors, and structures. "The gig economy", "the semiconductor industry", "the Middle East balance of power", "the housing market in Tokyo", "the open-source software ecosystem", "climate change politics", "K-pop as a cultural industry" all map here.

Key distinctions:
- vs. Organization: a specific company is an Organization; the industry it operates in is a System
- vs. Event: a specific crisis is an Event; the systemic conditions that produce crises are a System
- vs. Concept: "capitalism" as a theory is a Concept; "the American economy" as a living system is a System

## Research Dimensions

These dimensions define what to investigate and ensure adequate coverage of. They are **not** chapter headings — the report's TOC should be crafted fresh for each subject with unique, compelling titles. A dimension can span multiple chapters, be woven into other sections, or be combined with another dimension. The goal is coverage, not section-matching.

### One-Line Definition

What is this system? Define its boundaries — what's inside, what's outside. Who participates, and what does it produce or regulate?

### How It Works

The mechanics of the system. This is the most important section — the reader needs to understand the machinery before anything else makes sense.

- **Structure**: How is the system organized? What are the main components, layers, or sectors?
- **Flows**: What moves through the system? Money, goods, information, people, power — whatever the relevant currency is.
- **Incentive structures**: What motivates the key actors? What does the system reward and punish?
- **Feedback loops**: Where are the self-reinforcing dynamics? What amplifies itself? What are the balancing forces?
- **Bottlenecks and leverage points**: Where are the choke points? Where does a small change produce large effects?
- **Rules of the game**: What formal and informal rules govern the system? Regulations, norms, standards, unwritten codes.

Write this like you're explaining the game board and rules before describing any particular match.

### Key Players

Who are the major actors in this system, and what do they want?

For each major player (which may be individuals, companies, governments, or groups):
- What role do they play in the system?
- What are their interests and motivations?
- How much power or influence do they have?
- What is their current strategy or posture?
- How do they interact with other key players?

Also cover:
- **Power dynamics**: Who has leverage over whom? Where are the dependencies?
- **Alliances and rivalries**: What are the major alliance structures or competitive dynamics?
- **Newcomers and disruptors**: Who is trying to enter or change the system?

### Origin and Evolution

How did this system come to be, and how has it changed?

- **Formation**: When and how did this system emerge? What forces created it?
- **Key transitions**: What were the major structural shifts? What caused them?
- **What's changed vs. what's stayed**: Some aspects of systems are remarkably persistent. What are the durable features, and what has been in flux?

This section is shorter than in other archetypes because systems often have long, gradual histories that matter less than understanding the current mechanics. Go deeper only if the historical context is essential for understanding the present.

### State of Play

The current condition of the system. For systems, this is a primary section — often the main reason the user is researching.

- **Current state**: What's happening right now? What are the dominant dynamics?
- **Vital signs**: What are the key metrics or indicators? Where do they stand?
- **Active tensions**: What are the unresolved forces pulling the system in different directions?
- **Recent shifts**: What has changed in the last 6-12 months? What's new?
- **Mood and momentum**: What's the prevailing sentiment among participants? Is the system in an expansionary, contractionary, or transitional phase?

### Forces and Trends

What forces are shaping the system's future?

- **Structural trends**: Long-term forces that are reshaping the system (technology, demographics, regulation, climate, etc.)
- **Cyclical dynamics**: Recurring patterns — boom/bust cycles, seasonal patterns, pendulum swings
- **Wild cards**: Low-probability, high-impact events that could disrupt the system
- **Emerging dynamics**: New forces that are just beginning to be felt

### Verdict

Analytical synthesis of the system.

- **Health assessment**: Is this system functioning well? For whom? At whose expense?
- **Stability analysis**: Is the system stable, fragile, or in transition? What would it take to break it?
- **Winners and losers**: Who benefits from the current configuration? Who is disadvantaged?
- **Trajectory**: Where is this system heading? What are the plausible scenarios?
- **Intervention points**: If someone wanted to change this system, where would they push? What are the realistic levers?
- **The bottom line**: What should the reader understand about this system that isn't obvious from the headlines?

## Coverage priority

Spend the most space on the evidence that explains the subject and changes the reader’s judgment. Assign research ownership dynamically from the coverage map; do not force a fixed agent split.

## Coverage ledger mapping

Turn every relevant dimension above into one or more ledger coverage items with
an explicit priority, decision relevance, completion criterion, and evidence
target. If a dimension is relevant but cannot be resolved, keep it in the
ledger as `status: gap` with a concrete `gap_impact`; never omit it silently.
