# Editorial modes

Choose at most one dominant mode. Use its craft traits; do not imitate a living writer's distinctive voice.

## Analytical

Best for companies, technologies, markets, and policy. State the judgment early, compare alternatives on consistent criteria, quantify when evidence permits, and make assumptions visible.

## Narrative

Best for people and events. Build around consequential scenes that sources document, then connect them to the larger explanation. Never invent dialogue, setting, motive, or chronology.

## Explanatory

Best for concepts and systems. Move from a concrete example to the mechanism, then test the model against exceptions and limits.

## Investigative

Best for disputes and accountability. Separate allegation, documentary evidence, response, and independent verification. Give the subject's strongest supported answer, not a token rebuttal.

## Historical

Best when sequence and change explain the present. Use period-appropriate sources, avoid hindsight certainty, and distinguish contemporary interpretation from later scholarship.

## Critical

Best for works, products, and ideas. Describe the object accurately before judging it. Define the criteria, compare credible alternatives, and show where reasonable readers may disagree.
